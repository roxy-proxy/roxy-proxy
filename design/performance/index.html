roxy

Performance

On ordinary HTTP traffic roxy is on a par with Envoy. As a gateway it handles about 30% more small requests per core, with a lower median latency at both light and heavy concurrency, and moves the same bytes per second on large bodies for about a quarter more CPU (#104). As an egress proxy it is ahead at every size, by a third to a half per core. Doing TLS interception it handles about 20 times the small requests per core of mitmproxy, falling to about 3 times on 1 MiB bodies, with a median latency in milliseconds rather than hundreds of milliseconds.

What an addon costs

An addon is a fixed charge on the throughput roxy would otherwise have, before the addon's own work, and a charge per byte on the bodies that stream through it. A WASM layer that forwards everything unchanged costs about half of the small-request throughput and most of it on large bodies; a service layer doing the same costs about 60% on small requests and most of it on large bodies, plus whatever the sidecar and its network hop add. Most of the body cost is common to both kinds and is paid even when the layer leaves the body untouched: it is in the lane pipeline between the stages (#558), with the service's socket on top.

In CPU terms, roxy spends about 100 µs on a small exchange with no addons and a do-nothing layer of either kind adds roughly another 100 µs: the calls into the host for WASM, a dozen messages over a second socket for a service. So a layer that does T µs of work per request makes an exchange cost about 200 + T µs, and four cores give roughly 4,000,000 / (200 + T) requests per second: 20,000 with no work, 13,000 at 100 µs, 4,000 at 800 µs. A service layer adds the sidecar's own CPU on top. A body through a layer adds about 3 µs per KiB for WASM and 4.5 µs per KiB for a service, so a 1 MiB response through a do-nothing layer costs roxy 4 to 5 ms against about 0.6 ms with no addons.

What is not measured

No run uses body rules, capture, body digests or a real addon. A digest costs about one core per 400 MB/s on a CPU without SHA-NI, which is why it is opt-in.

These figures come from one machine, measured against Envoy and mitmproxy and with a do-nothing addon of each kind; the detail on the fixed cost is in #403 and #425. The addon rows were re-taken after the lane pipeline (#558); the Envoy and mitmproxy rows were not.
